Part I · the election up to the count · text of Oct 26, 2026

Summary

For 186 days AFOS recorded, in parallel, the price of the election winner contract on Polymarket, the national polls registered with the TSE and press coverage, in a daily edition and on a public dashboard. This report brings these series together and compares them with the official result of both rounds. It describes what each instrument showed; it does not rank the instruments.

  1. The market switched favorites several times. Between Apr 22 and Oct 24, Flávio Bolsonaro closed ahead of Lula in the winner contract on 63 of 183 days. Lula's highest close was 66.50, on Aug 1; his lowest, 11.50, on Oct 9.
  2. Up to the first round, runoff polls more often had Lula ahead. Of the 111 national polls with the Lula versus Flávio matchup published from Apr 22 to Oct 3, 62 had Lula ahead and 41 Flávio.
  3. In the first round, the range of the polls contained the result, and the market got the order wrong. The 9 national polls on the eve ranged from Lula +5.1 to Flávio +4.0; the count gave Flávio +1.87. The 2nd-place contract paid 57.50 on Flávio; Lula came 2nd.
  4. In the runoff (second round), market and polls pointed the same way, and the count went the other way. On the eve, the contract paid 83.40 on Flávio, and 9 of the 10 national polls had him ahead, in a range from Lula +0.5 to Flávio +3.0. The count gave Lula +3.58, outside the range. After the first round, 45 of the 49 national polls with the head-to-head in total votes had Flávio ahead or tied.
  5. On runoff night, the price moved against the count. From 19:46 to 20:21, Flávio led the TSE count, peaking at 50.49%. In the same interval, Lula's price went from 67.20 to 89.10.
  6. The money was contested, not owned. In September, a group of 4 wallets with signs of coordinated activity accounted for 43% of the net aggressor flow against Lula from Sep 12 to 21. In October, the flow switched sides three times and that group fell to 5.4% of the aggressor volume against Lula.
How to read this. A contract price runs from 0 to 100 and pays 100 to whoever is right. It is not voting intention: a statistical tie in the polls can coexist with a price of 80. Poll margins are in points of total votes when the pollster published the total; the TSE result is in valid votes, and this is stated in each comparison.

The Polymarket presidential book is of the negRisk type: the candidates are mutually exclusive, and buying a candidate's NO is equivalent to buying all the others. This allows paired positions (for example, Lula YES with Flávio NO) that reduce risk without changing the direction of the bet, which is why, in the flow studies, selling a lot does not mean betting against.

2The 186 days in the market

The contract opened the period almost tied and had three phases: Lula ahead from mid-May to September, with the highest close on Aug 1; Flávio ahead from Sep 10; and, after the first round, Flávio comfortably ahead, up to the eve of the runoff.

Six other national polls from the period did not publish the pair in total votes and are left out of the figure.

4Market and polls side by side

The two instruments measure different things, and the difference has a known shape: a statistical tie in the polls turns into a high price for whoever is slightly ahead, because the contract pays all or nothing. In 2026, this showed up clearly after the first round. With the national polls showing Flávio 1 to 5 points ahead, the contract reached close to 89 on Flávio, on Oct 9, and ended the eve at 83.40.

The reading the data support is that of two instruments that, in the runoff, pointed the same way with very different intensities: the polls, to a tight race with Flávio slightly ahead; the market, to Flávio as the clear favorite. The count gave Lula a margin of 3.58 points of valid votes, a margin that none of the 10 pollsters on the eve showed.

5Runoff night

Voting, scheduled to end at 17:00, was extended until 19:00 by an injunction from Justice Cristiano Zanin of the STF, at the request of Lula's coalition, because of a lack of public transport in cities in Bahia. The TSE president, Nunes Marques, limited the extension to the polling stations that were still open; the TSE justices met behind closed doors; the Electoral Public Prosecutor's Office spoke out against it; and the TRE-DF said it had not been notified. The release of results only began after 19:00.

At the first reading, at 19:35, with 8% of polling stations, the contract was already paying 69.50 on Lula. From 19:46 to 20:21, Flávio led the count, peaking at 50.49% of valid votes with 21.57% of polling stations. In that interval Lula's price went from 67.20 to 89.10, with a low of 63.40 at 19:53. Lula moved ahead at 20:24, with 47.62% of polling stations, and the tabulation ended at 23:27, with 51.79%.

The next day, Flávio Bolsonaro did not recognize the result and announced that he will ask the TSE to revoke the winning ticket's mandate; the PL, his party, recognized the result in a statement, with reservations. Governments of more than 25 countries congratulated the winner; the United States government had made no official recognition by the night of Oct 26.

6The money: three flow studies

Throughout the cycle, AFOS carried out three studies on who bought and sold in the Lula and Flávio contracts, based on public data from the platform and the Polygon network. The yardstick common to all three: flow is not impact. Whoever trades the most is not necessarily whoever moves the price the most, and similar behavior across wallets is statistical evidence, not proof of coordination.

September: the turn toward Flávio (Aug 25 to Sep 21)

Lula's contract went from 0.625 to 0.385 and Flávio's from 0.351 to 0.610. A group of 4 wallets, with an overlap of markets that would hardly occur by chance (less than 1 in 1 million against a random control) and administrative operations in sequence between the accounts, was the largest identified net buyer: 17.0%, 48.6% and 43.3% of the net aggressor flow in the three windows. In the attributable part of the price move, the group explains 10% to 21%, 16% to 44% and 31% to 65%, depending on the method. The persistent price shocks came 2 to 10 hours after public polls in the same direction. The calculations were redone from scratch with code separate from the original: of the 41 verifiable claims, 28 reproduced exactly, 10 had their wording adjusted and 3 were corrected before the final version.

The largest position on Lula (October 2025 to October 2026)

A single wallet was, from November 2025 to July 2026, the largest holder of Lula YES. In May, a large-position tracker flagged a "whale" of about US$ 328 thousand on Lula, and the article (Bitcoin World) has four figures that match this wallet, without naming it. It bought for a year, almost only with limit orders, reached 1.06 million shares at a cost of US$ 505 thousand and, on its best day, Aug 1, was US$ 159 thousand above cost, without selling. On first-round night the contract fell from 0.37 to about 0.15. Four days later, on Oct 8, it sold everything at market in 41 seconds, at an average price of 0.126, and received US$ 133.6 thousand. With Lula's victory, those shares paid US$ 1 each, about US$ 1.06 million. The measured pattern is that of a high-conviction bettor who also earned rewards as a market maker; the data do not indicate advance information.

October: the flow switched sides three times (Oct 6 to 24)

Gross aggressor volume was 9.67 million shares against Lula and 10.65 million in favor; the balance, small next to the gross, was 978.7 thousand in favor of Lula. The September group stayed on Flávio's side, but fell to 5.4% of the aggressor volume against Lula, with 87% of its flow concentrated in the first six days. The balance in favor of Lula came from new wallets: of the 91 with a detailed position, 17 had their first activity from Oct 1 onward and added up to 1.06 million shares in favor of Lula. The largest buy in the window was 423.9 thousand shares of Lula YES, on Oct 15. The largest sell was the exit of the largest position on Lula, described above, which the study classifies as the unwinding of a paired position, not as a bet against Lula.

None of the three studies identifies people, and none claims coordination, manipulation or use of inside information. In this report, wallets appear without address or nickname. In the dataset, runoff night has 24,784 trades with the wallets labeled A1 to A4295.

7The press

The press entered the series as a record of what was published each day, not as a forecast: 5,434 links in 187 editions. AFOS does not measure the accuracy of the press. What the series allows is a reading of sequence: in the flow studies, the persistent price shocks came after public polls in the same direction, and none could be confidently linked to information not yet released.

Two editorial rules stayed fixed during the cycle: a high-impact fact (arrest, court decision, testimony) only enters with two sources from different media groups, and the date of the fact comes from the body of the article, not from the publication timestamp.

8Method and limitations

  • Price. Published only after two readings agree; the series of closes uses the last point recorded each day. Books with few trades can have a wide range within the day.
  • Polls. National polls with the head-to-head in total votes; pollsters that published only valid votes are left out of the figures and are in the dataset with a note. Different polls do not form a series: methodology, base and margin vary.
  • Result. Valid votes, from the final TSE file. Comparisons between poll totals and ballot-box valid votes are stated at each point.
  • Wallets. Public data from the platform and the Polygon network. Coverage of runoff night trades is complete from 20:15 (Lula) and 20:26 (Flávio), partial before.
  • Errata. Four figures published in the dataset were corrected during the cycle, all recorded in ERRATA.md, with the old and the new value.

9What the divergence showed

Agreeing is not confirming. From April to September, market and polls frequently disagreed: runoff polls more often had Lula ahead, and the contract switched favorites several times. In the runoff, the two came to point the same way, and that is when they were wrong together. On the eve, 9 of 10 national polls had Flávio ahead and the contract paid 83.40 on him; the count gave Lula a margin of 3.58 points.

The two instruments were not independent. In the flow studies, the price shocks that lasted came 2 to 10 hours after public polls in the same direction. The market read the polls and amplified what they showed: a lead of 1 to 5 points became a price of 80 to 89. When one instrument feeds on the other, agreement between them adds little.

The width of the divergence is information. In the first round, the polls on the eve ranged from Lula +5.1 to Flávio +4.0, and that wide range contained the result. In the runoff, the range narrowed to Lula +0.5 to Flávio +3.0 and left the result outside. A narrow range gives more confidence, not more certainty.

The price is fast, not infallible. On counting night, the contract passed 50 on Lula before the first reading and reached 89.10 while Flávio still led the count. On the eve, the same contract paid 16.50 on Lula. It reacts quickly to what arrives, but it does not know beforehand.

Big money did not decide the price. The largest position on Lula sold everything after the first round, close to the bottom. The most active group of wallets in September lost weight in October. The flow switched sides three times. In none of the three studies did volume alone explain the move.

The closing message. No instrument alone got the 2026 runoff right. What makes it possible to say how much each one missed, and when, is having the record of all three published before the result, with date and time. Market, polls and press measure different things, and the most useful reading is that of all of them side by side, with the divergence between them treated as data.

The question of jurisdiction

Organizing the vote is the responsibility of the electoral courts, and the decision came from an STF justice, in an individual injunction, in the middle of election day. The point was raised by observers, the press and the parties, with differing assessments:

  • OAS (preliminary report, Oct 27): "unprecedented situation"; the injunction and "the TSE's delay in ruling caused confusion"; the mission urged the parties involved to prevent a repeat and recommended defining when the STF may intervene in electoral matters.
  • Carter Center (Oct 27): the last-minute extension created "a challenging operational environment", with contradictory decisions between the TSE and the STF.
  • Gazeta do Povo (news report): the decision "increased legal uncertainty in the election", because of the partial closing of polling stations and the difficulty of applying it uniformly.
  • Fernando Neisser, FGV Law School (ConJur, Oct 26): the difference in voting hours could perhaps serve as grounds for a legal challenge, but the margin of more than 4 million votes makes annulment unfeasible.
  • Flávio Bolsonaro (letter, Oct 28): called the decision an "illegitimate and illegal action by Lula's former lawyer".
  • Fox News (Oct 26): treated the extension as a "key irregularity" that fueled uncertainty about the result.

The measured facts are two: the decision changed voting hours in part of the country on election day itself, and the votes recorded after 17:00 added up to less than 1% of the final difference. Judgment on jurisdiction rests with the bodies and experts cited; AFOS records the debate.

The "mathematical impossibility" claim rests on the growth in Lula's votes between the rounds. The TSE files, reproduced in the annex to this report, show what changed: turnout rose by 2,960,239 votes, and abstention fell from 21.08% to 19.22% of the electorate. The claim was amplified on social media by Elon Musk (section 16) and, on Oct 29, by Jason Miller, a Trump ally.

The arithmetic, in the TSE files. Between the rounds, Lula gained 10,376,694 votes and Flávio Bolsonaro 3,721,538: together, 14,098,232. This sum is exactly equal to the 9,316,747 valid votes given in the first round to the eliminated candidates plus the increase of 4,781,485 valid votes in the runoff. The increase in valid votes, in turn, is the higher turnout (+2,960,239) added to the drop in blank and null votes (-1,821,246). The three calculations close with no remainder. The files do not say who voted for whom; they say that each round's vote total is consistent with the other's.

14Leaving the country

On Oct 25, the Federal Police asked the STF for precautionary measures against Flávio Bolsonaro in an investigation into money laundering linked to betting companies (Pet 16.877/DF). Justice Alexandre de Moraes ordered the surrender of his passports within 24 hours and barred him from leaving the country. The deadline expired at 11:00 on Oct 27 without the surrender, and the Federal Police canceled his regular and diplomatic passports. (Agência Brasil, Poder360, Exame, CNN Brasil)

The decision was also contested: according to CNN Brasil, the measure drew criticism among the leadership of the Office of the Prosecutor General, and, according to Diário Carioca, Flávio's defense asked for Moraes to be removed from the case. On the other side, on Oct 27, federal deputies Lindbergh Farias, Rogério Correia and Ana Elisa (PT) asked for Flávio's pretrial detention and an Interpol alert (Poder360). As of Oct 31, none of these requests had been ruled on.

The "rescue"

On social media, supporters called the departure a "rescue". What is confirmed is the departure through Argentina and the entry into the US with the authorization of the American government. Reports that American agents occupied part of the Puerto Iguazú airport and that Santiago Caputo accompanied the operation come from anonymous sources (Clarín, La Nación) and are not confirmed; Rubio denies involvement. A video of military personnel on a plane, captioned as the "rescue", shows behind-the-scenes footage of a television series (Agência Lupa, Oct 30); another, old video was used in the same way (Aos Fatos). A request for or grant of asylum was not confirmed.

19Expert readings

Technology as a target. Jesús Delgado Valery, executive director of Transparencia Electoral, wrote in a LinkedIn post on Oct 30, at 10:28, that the Brazilian case confirms a regional pattern, "from both the left and the right": the challenge exploits the public's limited familiarity with the transmission and tallying of votes, confuses error with fraud and takes advantage of an asymmetry of time: "sowing doubts about an algorithm takes seconds. Refuting it requires weeks of expert reports". For him, "well-designed and auditable technology is not the instrument of fraud, but the best shield against it". The argument picks up his article in Latinoamérica21, of Jun 14, 2026, on Colombia.

The geography of the vote. Federico Bartalucci, a PhD candidate in economic geography at the University of Cambridge, wrote in a LinkedIn post on Oct 29, at 14:56, that "Brazil votes by region first" and that the 2026 change relative to 2022 occurred between cities and outlying areas: according to him, Lula's vote rose 1.7 points in the urban municipalities of the IBGE typology and fell by 1.2 to 3.1 points in the intermediate and rural categories. He places the reading in the literature on the Lula vote (Zucco, 2008) and on the urban and rural divide (Rodríguez-Pose, 2018). The regional figures he cites match the measurement in section 20; the municipal comparison with 2022 is his and was not redone by AFOS.

The judicial route. Fernando Neisser, a professor at FGV Law School, assessed in ConJur, on Oct 26, that the difference in voting hours could perhaps serve as grounds, but that the margin of more than 4 million votes makes annulment unfeasible: "I see no chance of success".

21What Part II adds to the reading

Three points from Part I gain a new source or measurement. The extension of voting, described on Oct 26 as decisions with different scopes at the STF and the TSE, was called an "unprecedented situation" by the OAS mission, which recommended setting limits on the STF's intervention in electoral matters; the votes after 17:00 added up to less than 1% of the final difference. The fraud claim that followed, amplified by Trump and Musk, can be checked against the TSE's public files, whose arithmetic is in section 13, and the defeated campaign's own coordinator said he had no evidence. And the market, which on the eve paid 83.40 on the defeated candidate, came to pay 4.15 on an annulment of the result.

22A note from AFOS to the reader

AFOS does not make forecasts: it shows, with open and auditable data, source and time, what the market, the polls and the press were saying at each moment, where they diverged and in which direction, with the context alongside. The market and the polls pointed to Flávio, and the press recorded that picture; the count gave Lula. This is part of the unpredictability inherent in every forecast and in the instruments that try to anticipate it. It is not for AFOS to assert or dispute whether there was fraud or a failure in the count; that role belongs to the institutions. The role of AFOS is to report the results of each round with the officially released data. On counting night, the market turned in minutes, before the count showed Lula ahead: those with money at stake react to each new piece of information, to protect what they have or to profit from the change. The market does not root for a candidate; the price follows the money. The polls of that moment were already out of date, and the press narrated each new piece of information.

And what about the voter? For the voter, the lesson is that, in these new times and with new tools and technologies, the electoral process moves in minutes, and the more information we have, the better we see the situation, without looking at a single point. That is why we look at all the signals together. In a process of this magnitude, no instrument offers certainty; what exists is the best probability available given the circumstances of each moment.

Annex

The official result of both rounds

Figures from the final TSE tabulation file and from TSE documents on ballot boxes, the same as in the AFOS dashboard cards and the dataset (results/ folder). Candidate percentage over valid votes; turnout and abstention over the electorate; blank and null votes over the votes counted.

Who won

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT), Brasil Pronto pra Mais coalition, was elected president in the runoff, on Oct 25, 2026, with 64,256,232 votes, 51.79% of valid votes. Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) received 59,826,041, 48.21%. Difference: 4,430,191 votes, 3.58 points. In the first round, Flávio Bolsonaro had come out ahead, with 47.03% against 45.16%.
