sinalDaSemana: "The previous issue opened by saying that the contract on Democratic control of the House had crossed the week with a single distinct value across 46 readings. This week it moved 2.00pp, from 92.50% at the close of 2 October to 90.50% at the close of 9 October, and it moved in steps: 92.50% through 6 October, 91.50% on 7 October, 90.50% on 8 October, and flat there in the final session. What makes the move relevant is not its size, it is that it survives normalisation. The sum of the pair came to exactly 100.00% at both edges of the window, so the implied probability fell the same 2.00pp that the price fell, and there is no book spread to discount. That is the difference between this week and the previous one, in which the sum of the pair reached 101.00% midway and the implied figure for Democratic control of the House fell to 91.58% without the price moving a single tick. In the Senate the reading is the opposite and it requires both measures. The contract on Democratic control closed the week at 62.50%, 2.00pp below the 64.50% of 2 October, but the sum of the pair OPENED over the period, from 100.00% to 99.00%, and normalised the retreat is 1.37pp, not 2.00pp. The Republican side of the same book shows the mirror image: it rose 1.00pp raw, from 35.50% to 36.50%, and 1.37pp normalised. Two points raw and one point thirty seven normalised are not the same number, and the difference between them is the level of overpricing the book carried at one edge and did not carry at the other. And it was inside this week that the Senate Democratic contract touched 67.50% on 4 October at 23:30 UTC, which is the top of the entire series recorded since 14 April, before giving back five points by Friday's close."

methodology: "Prices read on the AFOS's own route with the country parameter and an uncached read, and confirmed by the capture lock: two readings separated by 8 minutes, released only if they agree within 0.20pp, with the two required timestamps distinct. The certified reading for this issue is 11 October 2026 at 21:25:28 UTC. The series for each contract is read in the database backup and never on the history route, because that route caps the window at 90 days: on the Senate contract that would hide 162 points and would make the real floor of the series, 39.50%, appear as 43.50%. Closes are the last point recorded on each day, and tops and floors are intraday, because a daily close hides extremes. The weekly delta is anchored at the close of the previous Friday, 2 October, which is the convention of this United States series. Any range distribution is cited only when the sum of the book falls between 95% and 105%, and it is cited normalised by the sum. The generic ballot average is a simple arithmetic one, unweighted, on a 30 day rolling window, with one round per pollster per wave by the hierarchy likely voter, registered voter, adults, and with a wave published under a different instrument kept out of the count and declared with the pollster's own wording. Book depth is not cited in this brief, by an editorial decision of May 2026."
