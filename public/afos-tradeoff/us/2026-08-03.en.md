sinalDaSemana: "This is the **first issue with a weekly window** in the United States series. Issue №1, on 31/Jul, was an opening piece: our market collection had only just been switched on, and reporting 'the week' with three days would have been selling coverage we did not have. From here on the window is the previous Monday to Friday, on the same cadence as the Brazil edition.\n\nThe 27-31/Jul window has one finding and it is the **absence of movement**. In the [contract that pays if Democrats take the House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026), the price marked **85.50% on every single day**, eight readings across six days of series. This is not a small oscillation: it is the same number, repeated. In the [Senate contract](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026), Republicans have been at **55.50% since 22/Jul**, thirteen days without moving.\n\nThe only price that moved was the Democratic side of the Senate, from 43.50% to **44.50%** on 02/Aug, already outside the window. It rose **without the Republican side giving ground**, and the effect is arithmetic: the two sides now add to exactly 100.00%, where they added to 99.00%. There was no change in the reading of who wins, there was a closing of the gap between the two ends of the contract itself.\n\nOn the other side, those who measure vote intention **do not agree**. Our house average for the House is **D+5.69**, across 26 polls from 23 pollsters in a 30-day window. But the distance between the highest and the lowest reading is **9 points**, from D+11 at Big Data Poll and Emerson College to D+2 at Reuters/Ipsos, RMG Research and Harvard/Harris. Two first-rank houses, CNN/SSRS and Quinnipiac, were in the field on exactly the same days, 23 to 27/Jul, and came back with D+8 and D+7.\n\nThat is the crossing of the week: **the price is standing still and those who measure the electorate are split by 9 points**. The two things measure different quantities and neither corrects the other, but the reading of where the uncertainty sits is direct. It sits in the declared ballot, not in the price. The set of eight contracts this panel follows holds **USD 16.74M**."

antiAvgIntro: "This brief publishes a polling average and **never subtracts it from the market price**. It looks like a technical detail and it is not: it is what separates a defensible reading from an invented number."

  rightValue: "direction and movement, never level"
  rightDetails:
    - "**The American case that proves the point: in 2012 Democrats won more votes for the House and fewer seats.** The distance between winning in votes and winning in seats can be entirely geography, that is, how the districts were drawn. Anyone subtracting the two measurements is treating that geography as if it were somebody's error"
    - "That is why the legitimate comparison is one of **direction**, did the two move the same way or opposite ways, and of **conviction**, did one move a lot while the other stood still. This week the answer is clean: the price did not move and the polls disagree with each other by 9 points"
antiAvgClosing: "It is worth declaring what weakens this issue, and there are two things. The **House series is six days long**, starting 29/Jul, and six days do not distinguish a stable contract from one that has not yet had a reason to move: any superlative here is 'of the series', with the start date declared, never 'of the cycle'. The Senate series is longer, starting 06/May, and it is the only one in this piece that supports a historical comparison.\n\nThe second is about collection, and it is larger. **The polling base was rebuilt today, before this issue went out.** Our reading of the source page was destroying 43 of the table's 352 rows, and the effect was not losing a row: it was one pollster's name swallowing the entire row of the previous one. Because of that the Morning Consult tracker never came to exist in the base and stayed out of the average. **No percentage was wrong, and that is precisely why no gate complained:** the defect was in the label, not in the number. Once fixed, the reading went from 307 to 351 rows and the average fell from D+6.00 to **D+5.69**, because the three recovered Morning Consult rounds mark D+3, D+3 and D+4, all below the previous average. **The consequence for anyone following the series: the D+5.57 published in Issue №1 came out of the defective reading and does not compare with the number in this issue.** The polling series starts here."

methodology: "**Market:** Polymarket, read through the platform's own proxy, captured every 30 minutes and with a guaranteed daily point even when the price does not move, so the series records time and not only change. Every price cited cleared the capture guard: two independent readings eight minutes apart, released only if they agree within 0.20pp. **Polls:** simple arithmetic average over a 30-day window, unweighted and excluding no pollster, built from an index that says which polls exist, while each number remains attributed to whoever produced it. When the same pollster publishes more than one subsample of the same round, only one enters, but different rounds from the same pollster each enter, which is why the window holds 26 polls from 23 pollsters. ⚠️ **Collection defect fixed in this round, and declared because it changed a published number:** the reading of the source table flattened multi-line citations so as not to cut a cell in half, and in that flattening it treated the abbreviated reference, which has no closing tag of its own, as though it were an open citation. The effect was to sweep away the line breaks up to the next citation and destroy 43 of the table's 352 row separators. The rows did not disappear: they were absorbed into the name of the following pollster. Fixed today, the reading went from 307 to 351 rows, the 51 corrupted labels went to zero and the average fell from D+6.00 to D+5.69. No percentage was wrong before or after, and that is why the value gates caught nothing: the defect was in the label. The form gate now refuses any pollster name containing a pipe, a percentage or a cell attribute. **Distributions:** a distribution only reaches this page when its bands add to between 95% and 105%. In this round the Senate seat count (99.20%) and the governorships (101.65%) clear it; the House seat count (110.90%), turnout (91.40%) and popular vote margin (148.25%) stay out. **Book depth is not cited:** low liquidity on Polymarket does not mean the price is wrong, because the market is arbitraged continuously, and putting that number on display produces a skewed reading. **What this brief does not do:** it does not forecast a result, it does not say which measurement is right and it issues no investment recommendation."
