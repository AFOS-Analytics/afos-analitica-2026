sinalDaSemana: "The week of 21 to 25 September separated two behaviours that are usually read as one. In the House, the binary moved 3.00pp over two sessions and then stopped: the Democratic price closed at 92.50% on Monday the 20th and repeated 92.50% at every subsequent close through Friday, without a single tick. In the Senate, the same kind of contract moved 6.00pp through Wednesday the 23rd, touched 66.50% and gave back 4.00pp, closing the week at 62.50%. The closing delta against the previous Friday is +2.00pp, and it hides the entire round trip. One book moved and stayed; the other moved and came back.\n\nThe second finding sits inside the Senate, and it inverts last issue's. There are two ways to buy the same outcome: the control binary and the sum of the ranges in the seat distribution. Last week those two readings converged, and the convergence came almost entirely from the binary travelling to where the distribution already was. This week they moved apart again, and the movement switched sides. The Republican binary shed 3.00pp, from 40.50% to 37.50%. The sum of the ranges that give Republicans a Senate majority shed 9.20pp, from 40.80% to 31.60%, and normalised by the book's own sum it shed 6.79pp, from 39.50% to 32.71%. What moved, this time, was the distribution, and it moved about two and a half times what the binary moved.\n\nThe practical effect is a distance of 4.79pp in implied probability, or 5.90pp in the price you pay, between two ways of buying a Republican Senate majority. It exists in books on the same event, at the same close, and it depends on no forecast of ours: it is arithmetic over published prices. For a trading desk, it is a distance between two instruments, and it is the most concrete measurement in this issue."

methodology: "Polymarket prices, read through the AFOS proxy with a live and uncached reading, and confirmed by a double reading eight minutes apart with a 0.20pp tolerance before any number goes live. A daily close is the last point recorded that day in the series backup, not the API reading, and the floors and tops quoted as intraday come from the 122 readings per range and the 50 to 58 readings per binary recorded during the week. The weekly delta is measured against the PREVIOUS FRIDAY's close, 18 September, which is this series' convention. A binary pair is reported raw and normalised by the sum of the two outcomes, and that sum is declared at both borders whenever it changes. A seat distribution is only cited when the ranges sum to between 95% and 105%: in this issue the House closed at 104.90% and the Senate at 96.60%, and any sum of ranges is normalised by its own book's sum before being compared with a binary. The convention for a Republican Senate majority is 50 seats or more, because the Vice Presidency breaks ties, and 50 is a range border in that book, which makes the sum exact. In the House the 218 threshold falls inside a range, so there the distribution is reported as an interval and never as a sum. The generic ballot is a simple arithmetic average, unweighted and with no pollster excluded, over a rolling 30-day window, with one round per pollster per wave by the likely voter, registered voter and adult hierarchy. Decimal point in all three languages."
