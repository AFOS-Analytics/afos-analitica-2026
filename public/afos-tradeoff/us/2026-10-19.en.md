sinalDaSemana: "The previous issue closed by naming the band of 215 to 219 Republican House seats and saying what to watch in it: if it got more expensive, the range in which the two House books can agree would widen; if it got cheaper, the range would close and the two books would start charging each other. It got cheaper, from 6.40% to 5.45% of the book's mass, and they charged each other. The binary contract on Democratic control of the House crossed the week of 12 to 16 October without moving a tick: 44 recorded readings, floor equal to top at 90.50%, amplitude of 0.00pp, and the same 90.50% at the close of each of the five trading days. The pair summed to exactly 100.00% at both edges of the window, so here raw and normalised say the same number and there is no overpricing to discount. And it was not a lack of money: USD 2.40M of new money entered the House book during the week, against USD 0.63M in the Senate book, without the price moving a tick. The market's other book on the same question did the opposite. The distribution of Republican House seat bands, which is the only contract that gives the seat dimension, reorganised its own mass on a large scale: the band of 205 to 209 seats went from 11.50% to 28.90% in raw price, which is an advance of 16.99pp of the book's share, and became the most expensive band in the whole of it, ahead of the band of fewer than 190 seats. Mass left both tails at the same time, and the three bands from 220 seats upward gave ground together. Measured on the same basis at both edges of the week, the share of the book sitting below the majority threshold went from 85.26% to 90.65%, and the share from 220 seats upward fell from 8.34% to 3.89%. The binary stood still at 9.50% the entire time. The two books neither add to nor subtract from each other, and this brief does not convert one into the other: the seats book carries permanent overpricing, sums above 100% and is therefore read for its SHAPE and never for its level, while the binary, with the pair at exactly 100.00%, is read directly. What is being asserted is only the contrast in behaviour: one of the two books on the same question reorganised a quarter of its own mass, and the other did not move. In the Senate the reading is the inverse and requires both measures, as it already required in the previous issue for the opposite reason. The contract on Democratic control rose 2.00pp raw, from 62.50% to 64.50%, but the pair sum went from 99.00% to 101.00% over the period, and normalised the advance is 0.73pp. The Republican side is the cleanest proof of this: it closed the week at the same 36.50% at which it opened, 0.00pp raw, and its implied probability gave back 0.73pp. A contract that did not move a tick in 49 readings and whose implied probability retreated three quarters of a point."

methodology: "Prices read on the AFOS's own route with the country parameter and an uncached reading, and confirmed by the capture guard: two readings 8 minutes apart, released only if they agree within 0.20pp, with the two required timestamps distinct. This issue's certified reading is from 19 October 2026 at 18:33:27 UTC, and the real interval between the two timestamps was 8.0 minutes. Each contract's series is read in the database backup and never on the history route, because that route caps the window at 90 days and in the Senate contract that would make the series' real floor, 39.50%, appear as a higher value. The stretch of series the daily backup does not yet cover is closed with the route's intraday points and checked by positive control, series by series, comparing the two sources over the window in which both have data: in this issue the five series closed with identical points on both sides, and that is why every superlative here holds for the entire series and not only for what the backup already had. Closes are the last point recorded on each day, and top and floor are intraday, because a daily close hides extremes. The week's delta is anchored to the previous Friday's close, 9 October, which is the convention of this United States series, and the tool that computes it was required to reproduce the six numbers published in the previous issue before being used here. Any band distribution is only cited when the book's sum falls between 95% and 105% at the edges used, and it is cited as a share of the book, normalised by the sum at that edge. A band book summing above 100% carries overpricing, and this house tests where it sits by comparing the normalised share of the centre against the raw sum along the series: if the two move together, the excess is in the tail and normalising uniformly does not remove it. Measured across 940 captures of this book, the correlation is 0.267, that is, the excess is approximately uniform, and in that case normalising preserves the internal proportion. That is why the distribution here supports a statement about SHAPE and never about level, and that is why none of its shares is compared with the price of a binary contract. The generic ballot average is a simple arithmetic one, unweighted, in a 30 day rolling window, with one round per pollster per wave by the hierarchy likely voter, registered voter, adults, and with a wave published under another instrument kept out of the count and declared with the pollster's own wording. Book depth is not cited in this brief by an editorial decision of May 2026."
