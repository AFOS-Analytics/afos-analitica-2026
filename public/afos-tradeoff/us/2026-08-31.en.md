methodology: "AFOS reports the three signals separately: prediction market price, generic ballot polls with sample size and field dates, and press coverage from a fixed list of outlets. It does not build a composite index and does not subtract one quantity from another.\n\nEvery published price passes two independent readings eight minutes apart, and is only released when they agree within 0.20pp. This issue uses the reading of Aug 27, 17:28 BRT, with an approved verdict. Weekly changes use each day's CLOSE, not the first and last point in the window, because the second choice changes the number without changing the fact. The House series begins on Jul 29, 2026, when collection was switched on, so any superlative about it is always 'of the series', never 'of the cycle'.\n\n⚠️ Two caveats specific to this issue, and both weaken readings that would otherwise be tempting. First: the generic ballot base changed size three times inside the covered week, from 11 to 22 and back to 17 polls, because the 30-day rolling window moved across a period of uneven collection. No new field poll has entered since Aug 17, so the +0.18pp change in the average is entirely window composition, and calling it a move in the electorate would be false. Second: the price implied by the seat distribution is derived from thin bands, and the residual band at 51 or more absorbs the noise of the others, so the normalised aggregate carries a drift of roughly 0.20pp of its own that corresponds to no change in expectation.\n\nA distribution is only published if its bands sum to between 95% and 105%. In this issue the Senate sums to 98.05% and the House to 101.65%, both inside. The turnout contract summed to 92.30% in the confirmed reading and is therefore not published as valid in this issue, only recorded. Accumulated USD volume is cited as context and is not guidance, and is measured at the same instant as the price; book depth is not cited, by editorial decision of May 21, 2026. Code and method are open under Apache 2.0."
