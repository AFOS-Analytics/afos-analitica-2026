sinalDaSemana: "The week produced no votes and the two [Senate](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-senate-in-2026) books moved anyway, on different days, and the result is this issue's finding. On Monday both were paying **49.50%** on their own party, a pair summing to **99.00%**. On Wednesday the Democratic contract rose to **50.50%** and the pair began summing to **100.00%**, the only day of the week the two books agreed exactly. On Thursday the Republican contract rose as well, to **50.50%**, and the pair closed at **101.00%**.\n\nWhat this describes is a change of sign. On Monday the Republican book, paying 49.50% on the Republicans, implied **50.50%** for the Democrats, which is **above** what the Democratic book was paying. By Thursday the relationship is inverted: the Democratic book pays 50.50% and the Republican one implies **49.50%**. The disagreement between the two is the same size, **1.00pp**, with the opposite sign. This is not the market changing its mind about who controls the Senate; it is two separate books arriving at the same place along paths that do not talk to each other.\n\nIn the [House](https://polymarket.com/event/which-party-will-win-the-house-in-2026) the Democratic contract made a round trip, from **88.50%** on Monday to **87.50%** on Tuesday and Wednesday, and back to **88.50%** on Thursday. Thursday's close is identical to Monday's, **0.00pp**, and anyone reading only the endpoints does not see the dip. The Republican side rose **1.00pp** and stayed there.\n\nInside the seat distribution, the money moved in the same direction as the story: the band for **exactly 52 Republican seats gave up 0.80pp**, from 8.90% at Monday's close to 8.10%, and the band for the **tie at 50 rose 1.00pp**, from 11.50% to 12.50%. The tie is precisely the band the binary contract counts as Republican control and the distribution isolates. Money left the narrow majority and went into the scenario the vice president decides.\n\nThe house average on the generic ballot stands at **D+6.00pp**, across 17 polls from 13 institutes with field through **Aug 17**. It is against that yardstick that this issue's price is read, and the next section explains why the two quantities do not subtract."

methodology: "AFOS reports the three signals separately: prediction market price, generic ballot polls with sample size and field dates, and press coverage from a fixed list of outlets. It does not build a composite index and does not subtract one quantity from another.\n\nEvery published price passes two independent readings eight minutes apart, and is only released when they agree within 0.20pp. This issue uses the reading of Aug 27, 17:28 BRT, with an approved verdict. Weekly changes use each day's CLOSE, not the first and last point in the window, because the second choice changes the number without changing the fact. The House series begins on Jul 29, 2026, when collection was switched on, so any superlative about it is always 'of the series', never 'of the cycle'.\n\nThe generic ballot average is simple and unweighted, over a 30-day rolling window, and when one institute publishes more than one cut of the same round only one enters, by the hierarchy likely voter, registered voter, adults. Every poll enters with its institute, field dates, sample size and sample type, and this issue's base has field through Aug 17.\n\nA distribution is only published if its bands sum to between 95% and 105%. In this issue the Senate sums to 98.05% and the House to 101.65%, both inside. Accumulated USD volume is cited as context and is not guidance, and is measured at the same instant as the price; book depth is not cited, by editorial decision of May 21, 2026. Code and method are open under Apache 2.0."
