sinalDaSemana: "This is the last issue of the US series, and the first in which the contracts have a known outcome. Democrats won control of the House and the Senate, and the three binary contracts in this brief settled on November 4: Senate control and the election happening as scheduled that morning, House control at 13:34 UTC. Against the Friday, October 30 close, which is this series' anchor, Democratic control of the House went from 93.50% to 100%, and of the Senate from 70.50% to 100%. What the week shows, before the outcome, is the Senate's path. Between the Friday close and the eve of the vote, with no vote counted, the Democratic Senate control binary rose 6.00pp: 71.50% in the November 1 certified reading, 73.50% at 18:30 UTC on November 2, 75.50% at 20:00 and 76.50% at 21:30 UTC, the value of the eve certified reading at 22:06 UTC. On election day it moved between 74.50% and 77.50%, its highest value before the count, and at 23:00 UTC on November 3 it stood at 75.50%. From there it covered in hours what it had not covered in weeks: 88.50% at midnight UTC, 98.30% at 01:30, 99.80% at 04:30 and 100% at 09:00 UTC on November 4. The House contract held at 94.50% from the eve until 20:00 UTC on election day, then rose to 96.50% at 23:30, 98.20% at 00:30 and 99.80% at 02:00 UTC. Both seat-band books confirm the outcome by their shape. In the Senate book, the band of 47 Republican seats or fewer, which settled yes, was the most expensive in the book at the October 30 close, with 47.88% of the mass, and on the eve, with 55.13%. In the House book, the band of fewer than 190 Republican seats, which also settled yes, was the most expensive in the book on October 30, with 40.62%. One outcome does not grade a price: 76.50% on the eve left roughly one chance in four for the other side, and a single election cannot say whether that was too much or too little. What remains is the dated record, written before the vote, that anyone can check."

methodology: "This issue closes the weekly US series. Settlement prices are the ones the market itself published when closing each contract, and the capture guard, which requires two readings 8 minutes apart agreeing within 0.20pp, recognized the three watched books as settled in both readings of November 9, at 23:46 and 23:54 UTC. The eve record is the certified reading of November 2 at 22:06 UTC. Each contract's series is read from the database backup and never from the history route, because that route caps the window at 90 days and on the Senate contract that would hide the start of the series, which is April 14. Closes are the last recorded point of each day, and highs and lows are intraday. The week's delta is anchored on the prior Friday close, October 30, which is this series' convention, and the tool that computes it was required to reproduce the numbers published in the previous issue before being used here. For settled contracts, the final value of the delta is the settlement price, 100% or 0%. A band distribution is cited only when the book's sum falls between 95% and 105% at the edges used, and it is cited as share of the book, normalized by the sum at that edge: the Senate seat book summed 101.30% on October 30 and 104.30% on the eve; the House book summed 99.70% on October 30, and so it is cited only at that edge. The generic ballot average is a simple arithmetic mean, unweighted, over a 30-day window that closes on election day, with one round per firm per wave by the hierarchy likely voters, registered voters, adults, and with waves published under a different instrument, such as the preference for which party should control Congress or the ballot with the names of the district's candidates, kept out of the calculation and declared in the firm's own wording. The seat count cited is NBC News's on November 9, with Associated Press data, and it is a projection, not a certified result. Book depth is not cited in this brief, by an editorial decision of May 2026."
