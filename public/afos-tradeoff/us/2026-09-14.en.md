antiAvgClosing: "Inside the market itself there is a pair of numbers that can be set side by side, and it is in the Senate. The binary pays **48.50%** on Republican control. The seat distribution gives another number for the same question, provided a rule that is not written in the table is applied: a 50-seat tie counts as Republican control, because the vice president breaks ties. Adding the 50-seat band to the bands of 51 and above gives 39.70%, and normalizing by the sum of the eleven bands gives **40.43%**. Without the rule, the calculation would use only the bands of 51 and above and the distance would come out at 20.29pp, two and a half times the real one. That is why the two books are comparable in the Senate and not in the House: there the control threshold falls on a border between bands, and in the House it falls inside the band of 215 to 219 seats, which contains 218. This is a fact about how the contracts were cut, permanent and independent of the election. What changes from week to week is the distance, which in this issue was 8.07pp."

methodology: "AFOS reports the three signals separately: prediction market price, generic ballot polls with sample and field dates, and press coverage from a fixed list of outlets. It does not build a composite index and does not subtract one magnitude from another.\n\nEvery published price goes through two independent readings eight minutes apart, and it only goes out when they agree within 0.20pp. The most recent reading with an approved verdict is from Sep 13, 17:13 BRT. The changes in this issue start from the Sep 4 CLOSE, the last one the previous issue published, and run to the Sep 11 close, so that every price move belongs to exactly one issue, including what happens between Friday and Monday. The close is the last point recorded in the day, with the day counted in UTC, and new money uses the same two boundaries. When the text gives a range and a number of readings, the calculation covers every point recorded from Sep 7 to 11. The House series begins on Jul 29, 2026 and the Senate series on Apr 14, 2026, so any superlative about them is always 'of the series', with the start date, and never 'of the cycle'.\n\nWhen the two sides of a binary contract do not sum to 100, this brief publishes the raw and the normalized value side by side, because the difference between the two is exactly what distinguishes a point that changed hands from a point that appeared in the sum. A distribution is only published if its bands sum to between 95% and 105%. In this issue the Senate distribution sums to 98.10% at the Sep 4 close and 98.20% at the Sep 11 close, and the three scenarios in section 3 cover the second sum in full. The distance between the two Senate books normalizes the distribution by the sum of its bands and counts a 50-seat tie as Republican control. The generic ballot average is a simple arithmetic mean, with no weighting and no pollster excluded; when the same pollster publishes more than one sample from the same round, only one enters, by the hierarchy likely voters, registered voters, adults. Accumulated USD volume is cited as context and is not guidance; order book depth is not cited, by editorial decision of May 21, 2026. Code and method are open under Apache 2.0."
