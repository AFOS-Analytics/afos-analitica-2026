sinalDaSemana: "Issue №10 closed with a written, dated test, and the July 27-31 window returned a negative answer. That issue reported the market had begun pricing Flávio Bolsonaro **not reaching the runoff**, with his [third place contract](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-presidential-election-first-round-3rd-place) at 8.60%, and wrote down the criterion: if it held above 8% for one more window, it would stop being a thin-book artifact. **It did not hold for a single session.** It fell to 7.90% on Monday July 27 and closed Friday at **5.70%**, a fall of **2.20pp in the week**, while his [second place contract](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-presidential-election-first-round-2nd-place) rose **1.50pp**, to 78.00%. Recording the error comes before any new finding. This window's finding is in the [winner contract](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-presidential-election): Lula went from 62.50% on Monday to **64.50%** on Friday, up **2.00pp**, breaking the previous top of the AFOS series, which was 63.50%. The gap over Flávio went from 38.70pp to **40.10pp**, the **first reading above 40pp** in the database series, which starts on April 14 and has 108 days with data. The mechanics changed: the gap widened **with both names rising**, because Flávio also gained 0.60pp and closed at 24.40%. It is not deterioration of the challenger, it is demand for the favorite. Four national polls came out in the window and they **do not agree** on the first round gap: 9.0pp in [BTG/Nexus](https://divulgacandcontas.tse.jus.br/divulga/) on July 27, 9.1pp in AtlasIntel on July 29, **6.0pp in PoderData on July 30** and 9.3pp in Vox Brasil on July 31. An average of 8.35pp would hide that the outlier is a single one. On the macro axis, the 4.50% to 4.99% band of the [inflation contract](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-annual-inflation-2026) jumped 13.00pp in a single session, on July 28, the day the IPCA-15 came in at 0.06% and below projections. The presidential market has accumulated **USD 118.16M**. The following window concentrates on **August 5** the Copom decision, the end of the party convention deadline and the Quaest poll."

methodology: "AFOS Tradeoff aggregates three signals **without averaging them into a composite**: [Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-presidential-election) (USD-denominated, ~30min latency), polls registered with the [TSE](https://divulgacandcontas.tse.jus.br/divulga/) (declared intention, variable frequency) and 400+ press sources (event flow). When the three diverge, the divergence *is* the signal, not the consensus. USD volume is reported alongside the implied probability to separate conviction from artificial spikes. Liquidity (book depth) is not cited inline: low liquidity on Polymarket does not mean the price is wrong, the market is continuously arbitraged.\n\nWeekly deltas come from snapshots persisted in our own database, not from retroactive reconstruction, and use the **last point of each day**. This produces differences of hundredths relative to the AFOS Daily, which reports the **intraday capture**: the July 31 gap appears here as +40.10pp and in the Daily as +40.15pp, and Renan's price vs. polls distance as 5.20pp here and 5.15pp there. Both are correct within their own convention, and we record it because the alternative would be the reader finding out alone.\n\nMethod note on superlatives: the public history API caps the query at 90 days, so any 'largest of the cycle' checked through it measures 90 days. The maximum claims in this issue were checked directly against the database, over **108 days with data between April 14 and August 2**, and are written as **of the series**, never as of the cycle. What came before April 14 the series does not prove, and that is why no superlative here goes beyond that date."
