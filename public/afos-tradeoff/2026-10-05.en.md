sinalDaSemana: "This issue covers seven days rather than five: from Monday, 28 September, to the close of the first-round count on the night of Sunday, 4 October. Closing on Friday would leave out the day on which the [presidential contract](https://polymarket.com/event/brazil-presidential-election) moved more than in any full week of the series.\n\nThe gap between **Flávio Bolsonaro** and **Lula** widened from **16.50pp** at Monday's close to **72.60pp** at 23:30 UTC on Sunday, with Flávio Bolsonaro at **86.10%** and Lula at **13.50%**. That is **56.10pp** in seven days. Sunday's high exceeds by **48.80pp** any instant of the paired series recorded since 14 April, whose previous record was 23.80pp, on 21 September. From Monday to Friday the move was small, from 16.50pp to 13.40pp, with a low of **8.30pp** at 07:00 UTC on Friday, hours after the cancellation of the TV Globo debate. Almost all of the shift was left for the weekend: 16.00pp on Saturday, the day nine national polls were released, and 43.20pp on Sunday, the day of the vote and the count.\n\nThe Superior Electoral Court (TSE) closed the official tally on 5 October with **Flávio Bolsonaro 47.03%** and **Lula 45.16%** of valid votes, and the two contest the second round on 25 October. On the eve of the vote, five of the nine national polls put Lula ahead in the first round. The final distance, **1.87 points** for Flávio Bolsonaro, fell within the interval of the nine, which ran from 5.1 points for Lula to 4 points for Flávio Bolsonaro.\n\nThe side books reached resolution on the same Sunday. The first-round second-place book closed at **99.30%** for Lula and the third-place book at **99.40%** for Augusto Cury, the same order as the ballot box. The book on the party with the most Senate seats closed at **97.70%** for the PL, which won 19 of the 54 seats in contention. The presidential book took in **USD 13.41M** of new money over the window and reached **USD 169.90M** accumulated."

execSummaryIntro: "The week's theme is the passage from measurement to result. For five days the contract moved within the band of the previous weeks, while more than twenty national polls split over who led each round. This issue measures what each instrument showed before the result, and what changed when the ballot box became the reference."

trackRecord: "Issue №19 assigned about 55% to the base case, in which the last wave would repeat the split of the previous weeks, a simple majority giving the second round to Flávio Bolsonaro and a majority giving the first to Lula, with the gap within the band of 10pp to 24pp. Through Friday that is what happened: the five closes stayed between 13.40pp and 22.30pp, and the intraday low of 8.30pp fell 1.70pp below the edge of the band. methodology: "AFOS does not produce a weighted average and does not arbitrate between sources. It publishes the three signals side by side, contract price, registered poll and news flow, and reports the distance between them when it exists. A market price goes live only after two independent readings eight minutes apart that agree within 0.20pp, contract by contract, and the reference series are measured in the daily backup of the database and not in the public interface. This issue covers seven days, from Monday to the close of the count on Sunday, by editorial decision: the convention for the Brazil issues is Monday to Friday, and the week of the vote concentrates on the weekend the shift that defines the series. The window's Δ is Sunday's close minus Monday's, and the gap is measured at paired instants, both sides with the same timestamp.\n\nThe polls cited are those registered with the Superior Electoral Court, with protocol, sample, margin and field dates declared, and the result is that of the TSE's official tally, read from the court's public files. On the night of the count AFOS published hourly editions of the dashboard and summary, archived with the tally and the price of each hour and open on Hugging Face and Harvard Dataverse. The code and the method are open under the Apache 2.0 licence. Every factual claim in this issue links to its primary source."
