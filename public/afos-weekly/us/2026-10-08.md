  text: "**The Economist/YouGov** closed fieldwork on **October 5** and put Democrats ahead by **12** among 995 likely voters, the widest margin published this week. It is not in our average, and has not been since **September 8**. The reason is in the pollster's own tab report: that question is asked **using the names of the candidates running in the respondent's district**. A generic ballot asks which party you would back for Congress; a named ballot asks about two specific people. Different questions, and the named one tends to run wider, because a voter unsure about a party can still like a neighbour. Our average answers one question, so it takes only the rounds that asked it. The exclusion is recorded with the pollster's own wording in the published data file, and the rounds return the day the house does."

methodology: "Market prices in this issue are October 8 closes, the last point recorded that day in our daily database backup, compared with the October 1 closes that Issue No. 9 published. Both ends of each binary pair are printed raw and normalised, because a pair that does not sum to 100 moves one side without the other side trading. The polling average is a simple, unweighted mean of one round per polling firm per wave over a rolling thirty-day window, with the hierarchy likely voters over registered voters over adults when a firm publishes more than one. Rounds that asked a named-candidate ballot instead of the generic ballot are excluded by wave, with the pollster's own wording recorded in the published data file. We never subtract a market price from a polling margin: one is a probability of controlling a chamber and the other is a margin in vote share, and in 2012 Democrats won more House votes and fewer seats."
