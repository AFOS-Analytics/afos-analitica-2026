methodology: "Market prices run from the October 15 close, the last point recorded that day in our daily database backup, to the latest reading on October 22, certified at 20:56 UTC by two captures eight minutes apart; October 22 has not closed. Issue No. 11 printed 63.50% for Senate Democrats, the last point recorded before it went out; the day closed at 64.50% at 23:30 UTC, and this issue measures from that close. New money is measured over the same window. Both ends of each binary pair are printed raw and normalised, because a pair that does not sum to 100 moves one side without the other side trading. Series highs and lows are checked against the full series, which begins on April 14 for the Senate and July 29 for the House. The polling average is a simple, unweighted mean of one round per polling firm per wave over a rolling thirty-day window, with the hierarchy likely voters over registered voters over adults when a firm publishes more than one row in the aggregating index. The split of this week's polling change between new rounds and rounds leaving the window is computed both ways round, which is why each part is a range. We never subtract a market price from a polling margin: one is a probability of controlling a chamber and the other is a margin in vote share, and in 2012 Democrats won more House votes and fewer seats."
