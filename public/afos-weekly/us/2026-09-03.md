That is why this brief reports both prices in a pair, and why **we write that the market is paying 89.50%** rather than that the probability is 89.50%. The first observes a price. methodology: "AFOS reads three signals and does not merge them: prediction market prices, published polls, and press coverage. The house average of the generic ballot is a simple mean, unweighted, over a rolling thirty-day window, and when one institute publishes more than one cut of the same round only one enters, by the hierarchy likely voter, registered voter, adults. Rounds that a pollster has published and the index has not yet received are read from the pollster's own tables and carry a marker saying so; when the index catches up, the indexed row replaces ours. Market prices come from a certified capture, two readings eight minutes apart that must agree inside 0.20pp before anything is published. Band markets are published only when the bands sum between 95% and 105%; the popular-vote-margin market is currently above 150% and is collected daily for the series without being published as a reading. This brief never subtracts a market probability from a polling lead, because they are different quantities. Open source, Apache 2.0."
