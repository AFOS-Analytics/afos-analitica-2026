howToRead:
  title: "Why this brief never subtracts the prediction market from the polls"
  text: "The prediction market prices the **probability that a party controls a chamber**. A poll measures a **lead in vote points**. Subtracting one from the other gives a number with no unit, one that would change size if the poll used another scale, without anything happening. The concrete American reason it matters: in 2012 Democrats won more votes and fewer seats. The distance can be entirely about how district lines are drawn. This brief cross-reads **direction and movement**, never level."

methodology: "**Prediction market:** Polymarket, read through this platform's own proxy and captured every 30 minutes, with a guaranteed daily point even when the price does not move, so the series records time and not just changes. Every price quoted here survived a capture guard: two independent readings eight minutes apart, released only if they agree within 0.20pp. **Polls:** a simple unweighted average over a 30-day window, no institute excluded, built from an index that says which polls exist while every number stays attributed to the institute that produced it. Poll aggregators are deliberately left out, because averaging an aggregator would mean averaging someone else's model. **Press:** a fixed list of 22 outlets, chosen once and applied identically on every run, so no outlet is picked after the news breaks. Fifteen are read from their own RSS, which gives the outlet's canonical article link; the rest come through an aggregator, and those links are shown as headlines rather than addresses because the address would not be the outlet's own. AFOS does not classify outlets by political leaning. **Distributions:** a band market only reaches this page when its bands add to between 95% and 105%. The Senate seat-count market passes at 102.10%, the governors market at 101.40%, and the turnout market now passes too, at 98.15%, after failing a week ago. The House seat-count market is at 106.95% and stays off the page as probability. The popular-vote margin market adds to 151.00% and stays off as well, and its bands are not a partition in the first place: one of them is an 'any other outcome' band at 45.50% and the rest are cumulative, so that sum will never close, and is collected daily anyway because it is the one contract that would allow a direct comparison with the polls if its arithmetic ever closes. **What this brief does not do:** it does not forecast, it does not say which measurement is right, and it does not tell anyone how to vote."
