methodology: "**Prediction market:** Polymarket, read through this platform's own proxy and captured every 30 minutes, with a guaranteed daily point even when the price does not move, so the series records time and not just changes. Every price quoted here survived a capture guard: two independent readings eight minutes apart, released only if they agree within 0.20pp. The House series opened on July 29, so a high here means the highest of nine days, never of the cycle. **Polls:** a simple unweighted average over a 30-day window, currently 31 polls from 24 institutes with field dates from July 7 to August 4, no institute excluded, built from an index that says which polls exist while every number stays attributed to the institute that produced it. Poll aggregators are deliberately left out, because averaging an aggregator would mean averaging someone else's model. When an institute publishes several cuts of one round, one enters, by likely voters before registered voters before adults. **Press:** a fixed list of 22 outlets, chosen once and applied identically on every run, so no outlet is picked after the news breaks. Fifteen are read from their own RSS, which gives the outlet's canonical article link; the rest come through an aggregator, and those links are shown as headlines rather than addresses because the address would not be the outlet's own. AFOS does not classify outlets by political leaning. **Distributions:** a band market only reaches this page when its bands add to between 95% and 105%. Today the governors market passes at 101.00% and the turnout market at 100.70%. The Senate seat-count market fails at 105.75% and the House seat-count market at 109.50%, so neither appears as probability. The popular-vote margin market adds to 152.05% and is structurally incapable of closing, because one of its bands is an 'any other outcome' at 45.50% while the rest are cumulative; it is collected daily anyway, because it is the one contract that would allow a direct comparison with the polls if it were ever a partition. **What this brief does not do:** it does not forecast, it does not say which measurement is right, and it does not tell anyone how to vote."
