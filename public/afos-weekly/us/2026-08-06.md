howToRead:
  title: "Why this brief never subtracts the prediction market from the polls"
  text: "The prediction market prices the **probability that a party controls a chamber**. A poll measures a **lead in vote points**. Subtracting one from the other produces a number with no unit, which would change size if the poll were expressed on another scale, without anything happening in the world. In 2012 Democrats won more votes and fewer seats: the distance between the two measurements can be entirely a matter of how district lines are drawn. What this brief cross-reads is **direction and movement**, never level."

methodology: "**Market:** Polymarket, read through this platform's own proxy, captured every 30 minutes with a guaranteed daily point even when the price does not move, so the series records time and not just changes. Every price quoted here survived a capture guard: two independent readings eight minutes apart, released only if they agree within 0.20pp. **Polls:** simple unweighted average over a 30-day window, no institute excluded, built from an index that says which polls exist while every number stays attributed to the institute that produced it. Poll aggregators are deliberately left out, because averaging an aggregator would be averaging someone else's model. **Distributions:** a band market only reaches this page when its bands add to between 95% and 105%. The popular-vote margin market adds to 146.50% and stays off the page; it is collected daily anyway, because it is the one contract that would allow a direct comparison with the polls if its arithmetic ever closes. **What this brief does not do:** it does not forecast, it does not say which measurement is right, and it does not classify news outlets by political leaning."
