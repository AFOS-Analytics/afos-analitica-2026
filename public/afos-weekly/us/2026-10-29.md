methodology: "Market prices run from the October 22 close, the last point recorded that day in our daily database backup, to the latest reading, certified on October 30 at 00:28 UTC (October 29, 8:28 pm Eastern) by two captures eight minutes apart. New money is measured from the October 22 close to the October 29 close. Both ends of each binary pair are printed raw and normalised, because a pair that does not sum to 100 moves one side without the other side trading. Series highs and lows are checked against the full series, which begins on April 14 for the Senate and July 29 for the House and the schedule contract. The polling average is a simple, unweighted mean of one round per polling firm per wave over a rolling thirty-day window, with the hierarchy likely voters over registered voters over adults, and it counts only the question that offers party labels. Rounds that name each respondent's district candidates are listed in the data file and left out of the average; the New York Times/Siena round entered that list on October 22, after Issue No. 12 had printed D+7.71 with it inside, so this issue compares against D+7.61, the October 22 average under the same rule. We never subtract a market price from a polling margin: one is a probability of controlling a chamber and the other is a margin in vote share."
